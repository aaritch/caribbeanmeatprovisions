Halal certification looks straightforward on the surface — the certificate is either there or
it isn’t. In practice, the issuing body matters as much as the certificate itself, and not
all halal seals are accepted in every market.

## The four bodies you’ll see most often

- **JAKIM** (Department of Islamic Development Malaysia) — broadly accepted across the GCC
  and Southeast Asia.
- **BPJPH** (Indonesia’s Halal Product Assurance Agency) — required for products entering
  Indonesia, increasingly recognised regionally.
- **GAC** (Gulf Accreditation Center) — endorsed by the GCC; common for shipments routed via
  the Middle East.
- **HAK** (Halal Authority Korea) — useful where Korean export programs are involved.

## What to ask your supplier

1. Which body issued the certificate?
2. Is the certificate facility-level or product-level?
3. What is the expiration date, and how is renewal handled?
4. Will the certificate accompany every shipment or be available on request?

## Caribbean-specific considerations

The Caribbean Indo-Caribbean and Muslim foodservice trade primarily expects JAKIM- or GAC-
endorsed certifications. For premium hotel banquet operations, GAC is the safer default. For
retail and supermarket programs serving the Trinidadian and Guyanese markets, JAKIM is most
commonly preferred.
