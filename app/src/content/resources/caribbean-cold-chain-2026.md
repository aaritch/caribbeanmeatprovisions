The cold chain is the invisible part of meat distribution — until it breaks. In the last
twelve months, three shifts have meaningfully changed what foodservice buyers across the
Caribbean should expect from their suppliers.

## IoT temperature loggers are now table stakes

Two years ago, real-time reefer-container monitoring was a premium service. Today it’s
standard practice for any serious B2B meat importer. If your supplier cannot produce a
temperature record on demand for a contested lot, you should treat that as a red flag.

## Port-side staging has shrunk

Several Caribbean ports tightened reefer-plug allocation in 2025. The practical effect: less
buffer time between vessel discharge and customs clearance. Suppliers who pre-clear paperwork
and stage refrigerated trucking in advance materially outperform on lead time.

## Documentation packs are getting denser

Phytosanitary, halal, and origin certificates increasingly arrive bundled rather than as
separate PDFs. Make sure your QA team knows how to verify each component — buyers who only
spot-check the bundle have been blindsided by missing endorsements.
